Conceptual structure study

NOT ENGINEERED. NOT FOR CONSTRUCTION. This separate viewer mode is a visually realistic construction study, not a structural scheme, sizing exercise, analysis model or approval. No structural drawings, soil report, loading, site survey, wind/seismic parameters, jurisdiction or design-code inputs have been supplied.

The study assumes an RCC frame with masonry infill for illustration of a familiar Indian residential construction appearance. That is not a confirmed choice for this house, nor an assessment of an alternative masonry-bearing system. More realistic surfaces and solid members do not make the assumed system adequate.

Editable source and shared geometry

output\structure-settings.js centralizes architectural wall-axis references, selected station indices, colors and exampleSectionsMM. Column entries are X/Y section dimensions; beam entries are width/depth, all in millimetres. They must retain the EXAMPLE_UNVERIFIED designation. Edit them only as visualization examples, not to size a real house or order materials. The compact on-screen legend reports the actual configured dimensions.

output\structural-concept.js derives stations, floor/wall-top/monitor levels, slab and roof/wall references from the same review-data.js as all other modes. In the current model, local grade is +2 ft, FFL +4 ft and wall top +14 ft; this is not a surveyed datum. Regenerate the architectural export before changing that architecture. Wall solids and native section faces are copied without changing their vertices/openings; the materials and optional clipping apply only to the separate study.

Example RCC members and the retained native wall envelopes interleave at assembly joints; this is not a boolean-unioned construction take-off. A small rendering depth bias prevents coincident masonry/concrete surface flicker. It does not specify a real joint, bearing, infill separation or reinforcement detail. The authored texture scale uses approximately 230 x 75 mm visible brick faces with 10 mm visual mortar joints; it is not a brick specification or quantity model.

Specific unresolved geometric findings

The example 450-mm-deep perimeter downstands intersect the upper approximately 145 mm of all six bathroom vent apertures. This is a dimensional clash, not a structural analysis. Rear beam B-ref-08 intersects V_01_Rear_W, V1_01_Rear_W, V1_02_Rear_W and V1_03_Rear_W; right-side beam B-ref-05 intersects V_02_Right_N and V_03_Right_N. Orange boxes mark the actual overlap and aperture locations. Opening and member layout/head levels need coordinated engineering/architectural review; this model does not resolve them.

The 300-mm columns are wider than the existing wall envelopes. At perimeter stations their outer faces project approximately 36 mm beyond the original exterior facade; at thin-partition junctions they also project into adjacent room corners. This unfitted study does not change the approved external dimensions or certify retained room clearances. The frame must be laid out and checked professionally, not accepted because columns appear to sit on wall axes.

The full open hall/dining span and monitor/hip-roof transfers remain amber support questions, with no guessed central posts. No flat RCC roof slab or invented trusses were added beneath the approved hipped roof. Slab support, internal gravity support, wall/frame interaction, lateral behavior and foundations remain unresolved. Window/infill relationships also require assessment; the cutaway is not an instruction to build partial-height walls.

Door/window lintels, sill bands, masonry bonds/ties, infill separations, reinforcement and connection details are not designed or detailed. The brick surface is an appearance texture, not individually laid masonry or a quantity model. Bare openings in this study are not a claim that unsupported masonry heads are acceptable. All these relationships need professional detailing.

Indian construction references and asset provenance

References were used for system/material understanding only; no source photographs, drawings, proprietary meshes or texture files are shipped. All concrete/brick/mortar textures are small locally authored procedural maps, with generic concrete mottling, restrained formwork traces and running-bond brick scale.

  • IIT Kanpur / BMTPC, Earthquake Tip 17: How Earthquake Forces Affect Reinforced Concrete Buildings — distinguishes RC beams/columns and masonry infill, and explains why visible component relationships are not a substitute for engineered connections and a complete system. Not used to size this house.
  • IIT Kanpur / BMTPC, Earthquake Tip 22: Why Are Short Columns More Damaged During Earthquakes? — informs the explicit warning that window/infill interactions need engineering review, and that display clipping must not be mistaken for proposed partial-height walls. No retrofit, reinforcement or force prescription is adopted.
  • RKB Build Pro, RKB Standard Bricks — manufacturer lists a 230 x 110 x 75 mm clay-brick product; used only as a real Indian material-scale reference for the generic authored brick appearance. No product performance, quantity or suitability claim is made.

All study objects live in a separate hidden-by-default group with EXAMPLE/UNVERIFIED or architectural-envelope metadata. Exterior, Cutaway, Walk/Fly and Plan retain their approved geometry. Structure-only Walls, Cut walls and Roof outline controls do not edit the architectural master. Leaving Structure restores normal view preferences.

Native CAD / IFC and review boundary

Home.FCStd, Home.ifc, the parametric architectural inputs, source sketches and archives are retained unchanged. This branch does not add guessed structural objects to those architectural files and does not provide a structural IFC/CAD package. The editable conceptual source is the separate viewer module/settings. No structural quantities, loads, foundation sizes, reinforcement, safety factors or construction details are generated.

A licensed local structural engineer must determine the actual gravity/lateral system, column/wall/beam layout, bearing, member sizes, joints, slab system, foundations, soil response and roof/monitor transfer paths. In particular, the open central space and raised monitor must be resolved rather than assuming a rectangle drawn in a viewer is supported. Construction, demolition or procurement must not be based on this study.
