Jadhav Nivaas / wada-Konkan-inspired roof monitor

Concept review only. NOT FOR CONSTRUCTION. Neither visual enclosure nor a represented grille establishes burglary resistance, structural adequacy, weather-tightness or code compliance.

Retained and revised

The original single-storey footprint, hipped tiled-roof form, assumed pitch/overhang, monitor dimensions and roof heights are retained. The central monitor remains a high-level daylight/ventilation feature, not an upper storey or an open courtyard.

The previously open band is replaced by four dark-timber louver assemblies: framed bays and overlapping, outward-downward blades. Recessed fine metal grilles are a visibly separate, illustrative backing layer. Indirect light and air paths are intended between blades; actual airflow, daylight and wind-driven rain performance have not been established. No glazing is implied behind these louvers.

Reference reading and limits

  1. Nidhi Patel, Gharpedia: Wada / traditional mansions of Maharashtra. This professional overview describes region-responsive roofs, sloping clay tiles in higher-rainfall regions, timber craftsmanship/lattice screens, and courtyards for light and air. Those broad material and climatic principles inform the interpretation; the new monitor is not a copied historic detail or proof of historical authenticity.
  2. Rupa Raje Gupta, Wada of Maharashta, an Indian courtyard house form, Cardiff University PhD, 2007. The indexed abstract identifies a systematic study across Maharashtra's five traditional regions. Bibliographic/abstract context only: the repository returned HTTP 403, so the full thesis was not consulted and no louver, grille or roof specification is attributed to it.

A courtyard-based wada precedent is not treated as permission to remove this home's roof or change its approved plan. The louvered raised monitor is a contemporary conceptual interpretation of the user's chosen direction.

Editable model and version preservation

parameters.json contains the explicit monitor_enclosure assumptions. build_home.py generates eight named native roof components: four timber louver assemblies and four recessed grille assemblies. Their actual tessellated geometry is reused by Three.js and their solids are exported to IFC. Room doors/windows and their schedule are unchanged.

The preceding CAD/IFC and generation inputs are retained in archives\before-monitor-enclosure. Current files remain Home.FCStd and Home.ifc.

Professional specification still required

Have the local architect and structural/MEP professionals resolve supports/load path and wind uplift; member sizes, joints and fixings; grille material, gaps, anchorage, corrosion and security assessment; insect exclusion; flashings, drainage and wind-driven rain; required free ventilation/daylight; fire/egress; and safe cleaning/replacement access. No dimensions here are engineered or certified for those purposes.
